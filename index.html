    How the slip is worked out

    The old amount is first changed into US dollars at the rate of its time. Those dollars are brought forward with the US consumer price index to today's dollars, and then changed into the currency you chose at today's rate.

    The dollar is the bridge because it stayed on gold the whole time and has one unbroken price index from 1913. The result is purchasing power measured in American prices, not in German ones.

    Paper mark rates are quoted for particular months. Months in between are interpolated on a log scale and marked as such. In 1923 the mark could lose half its value within days, so any monthly figure is a rough guide. Other currencies use yearly averages.
